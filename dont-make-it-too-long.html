"Don't make it too long, no one will read it"

13th November 2026

Many of us who write, started because we figured out at some point that words have power to change the minds and influence the structures around us. Words create worlds.

I’m thinking of the manifestos that catalysed social movements, books that introduced new paradigms and stories that allowed us to imagine a different world before we built it. For better or worse, the one who holds the pen has agency to shape the world.

But these days there is no shortage of people broadcasting their visions for society - on instagram, Facebook, blogs, websites. The Internet is an inundation of noise, a sea of people claiming their truth is the better one and AI makes it even easier to spew text mindlessly into cyberspace.

As a result we are desensitised to text. We skim read, don’t bother. Texts have to become short form, bullet pointed and reduced to keep our short attention tethered. How many clients have told me “don’t make it too long, people won’t read it”. I don’t say they are wrong but I’m thinking - of course they don’t bother because most of it is junk.

But what if we write something that’s worth reading?

Instead of joining in the noise, racing to pump out more content, setting up an relentless newsletters and blogs schedules because we think thats what we have to do to keep up, I’m an advocate for doing less - but with more intention and precision.

The feeling of completing something well crafted, that took time. Something that you laboured over. That you struggled with. That you nearly finished and then started all over again. In your devotion to the work, you keep you chipping away and then finally, something exists that you feel proud of. That truly reflects YOU. I think chatGPT strips us of that joy of work. The satisfaction when you complete something that feels authentic. The pleasure of the process.

Instead we want everything as quick as possible at the click of the button, so we can move onto the next thing. And yet, I think with some redirection and prioritisation of energy it’s possible to shift the operating system.

For example I recently had a client who was spreading herself thinly in all directions - retreats, workshops, talks, private coaching - trying to develop all of them at once. I told her I felt she was a bit scattered and maybe needed to prioritise. She returned a few days later, telling me she had decided to cancel all of her workshops. It was a radical move, because this is where most of her immediate income and clients came from. But just pausing for a few weeks gave her time to focus deeply on developing the foundational philosophy that connected everything meaning as she moved forward she had greater confidence in her work because she understood it better. She reduced her output, but increased its value. It’s like going from being a restaurant with a 3 page menu to one that has just 2 dishes to choose from. But you know it's going to be the best homecooked meal you’ve had in a while.

Let’s call this the artisanal approach to work. Seeing yourself as an artist in your particular field - one who needs time to read, think, rest, develop ideas. One that knows their limits, does not subscribe to superhuman business tycoon standards, mindlessly pumping things out in all directions, but cares about the time it takes to put thoughtful work into the world. It is the temporality of a dedicated craftsman.

And once that happens, trust me, people will read it. Even if it's long.
